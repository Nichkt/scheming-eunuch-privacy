Scheming Eunuch privacy policy

Effective October 1, 2026

Scheming Eunuch is an Android app by Nicholas Ton: a comedic AI assistant, voiced as a sycophantic palace eunuch, that lives in a home-screen widget. This policy says what it does with information.

In short: there's no developer account, no ads and no analytics. Your chats stay on your phone, except that what you say to the assistant is sent to the AI provider you choose, using your own key. The developer never receives any of it.

What the app keeps on your phone

The app stores your conversation, a short running summary of it, the facts the assistant has noted about you (for example, that you like tea), your reminders and your settings (names, title, voice, briefing time). API keys are encrypted with the Android Keystore. None of this is sent to the developer, who runs no server for the app. App data is excluded from Android backups. You can erase memories and chat history in Settings, and uninstalling the app deletes everything.

What the app sends over the internet

Your AI provider. The app needs an AI provider to answer you. You pick one (OpenRouter, Google Gemini, OpenAI or Anthropic) and supply your own key. When you send a message, or when the daily briefing is written, the app sends that provider your recent messages, the running summary, the noted facts, the current local date and time, and the assistant's instructions. The provider handles this under its own terms and privacy policy and your account with it: OpenRouter, Google, OpenAI, Anthropic. Free models may use what is sent to improve their services. Without a key, the app works offline with canned replies and sends nothing.

OpenRouter sign-in. If you choose "Sign in with OpenRouter", your browser opens OpenRouter's sign-in page; OpenRouter then gives the app a key, which is stored encrypted on your phone. The app also downloads public lists of available models from your provider.

Court gossip (optional, off by default). If you turn it on and grant Android notification access, the app reads the title and text of notifications from other apps. They are kept only in memory, never saved to storage, and are sent to your AI provider only when you ask about notifications or for the daily briefing. You can turn this off in Settings or in Android's settings.

All of this goes over encrypted (HTTPS) connections.

Voice, alarms and other actions

Voice input uses your phone's speech-recognition service (on most phones, Google's), which returns text to the app; the app does not record audio itself. Spoken replies use your phone's text-to-speech engine. Alarms and timers are handed to your Clock app, and opening apps or web searches hands off to those apps. The app only lists installed apps so it can open the one you name.

What the app doesn't do

There's no sign-in to the developer, no advertising, and no analytics or tracking code. The app doesn't ask for your location, contacts, photos, camera or microphone. The developer doesn't collect, sell or share personal information. If you report a reply or send feedback, your email app sends that message, including the reply text and your phone model, to the developer.

Children

Scheming Eunuch is not directed at children and is intended for adults.
